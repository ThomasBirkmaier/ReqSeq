# ADR-0008, part: Search index

Part of [ADR-0008](../0008-in-memory-model-and-index.md). Read this part when you work on `query/search-index.ts`, `query/search-state.ts`, `commands/element/search.ts`; the decision itself is in the ADR.

- Index (`query/search-index.ts`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#323): MiniSearch 7.2.0 (MIT, no dependencies, no network access; ADR-0015), one document per element record, keyed by repository and file (files may share a UUID, SR-088). Fields: `title` (the property with the meaning `title`, D-059), `text` (the Markdown below the frontmatter without the destinations of links and images: the reader sees the label, and the `reqseq:` destinations of inline references would add four times the words of the texts at N-036) and `properties` (the values of every other key except `id` and the keys that hold relations, also inside lists and mappings, dates as ISO text, and for a choice also its name; type and status by the name the metamodel gives them, else by the key as written, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#80: SR-068 searches "Properties", and the tool shows those names; not also by the key, which mostly holds the same words and cost the build about a fifth more time). IDs are not indexed (150,000 random tokens would cost about 110 MB and 1 s at N-036); a query word in the form of a short ID (`SR-46fb54`, also with 12 digits) is compared with the records (type prefix and the end of the UUID), a word of at least 6 hex digits finds the UUIDs that begin or end with it, and a word that is the whole identifier of an element (the property with the meaning `identifier`, `SR-036`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#305; per index, a map built on first use) finds it; exactly, in any case, never fuzzy, and independent of how many digits the shown short ID has (SR-084). The identifier is a property value too, so its words match as words as well. Words are split at every character that is not a letter, mark or digit and compared in NFC and lower case; fuzzy search tolerates one edit in a word of 4 to 7 letters, two from 8 (MiniSearch's fraction 0.2), none below 4 and none for a query word with a digit (a number, a version or a part of an ID: `0000b3` would find `0000b1`; a word of the index with a digit can still be the typo of a query word without one, `sensorx` finds `Sensor1`); prefix search from 2 letters; a title match counts twice (BM25).
- Fields of single properties (SR-216, #1673): after `title`, `text` and `properties` the engine has one field `property:<key>` per property a search can be restricted to (`searchPropertyKeys`: every property of the metamodel that holds no relations and is not the title, without the keys `title`, `text`, `properties`, `id`, `type`, `status`), in metamodel order, holding the same texts of that key as `properties`, a choice also by the names of its values. A document keeps its property texts per key (`SearchDocument.parts`, flat `[key, text, ...]` in frontmatter order, a key only when it has a text) and MiniSearch reads the fields from it (`extractField`): `properties` is the texts of every part one per line, byte for byte the text it was before the parts, and a property the element does not hold is the empty text, never none, so field lengths and averages do not depend on the order documents were added in (a carried or cached index scores as a fresh build). The three fields of words keep their IDs, lengths and words, so a search in every field scores and orders as before (SR-216 AK4; compared on the generated workspace, below). A restricted search (`fields`, `propertyKeys`) scores only the chosen fields (BM25 summed over them, a title match twice, every property field once), so a word in an unchosen field neither finds nor ranks a hit (SR-216 AK1); an ID match counts only with `id`, and a word that can match nothing in the chosen fields finds nothing (before, it was left out). `properties` beside single properties is `properties`. A hit found in a property field names `properties`; its snippet comes only from the chosen properties (SR-216 AK5). The field lengths `lengthsOf` keeps are whole numbers, so a `Uint32Array` (same scores). Rejected: filtering the hits of a search in every field afterwards (unchosen fields would still rank, against AK1), one engine per property (memory), building property fields lazily (the first restricted search would miss SR-098), and fields per key only (the scores of a search in every field would change, against AK4). The search cache has format 4 (3: the texts of the documents, #1806; 4: the fields `type` and `status`, #1807); its digest covers the list of property fields and the Markdown properties.
- Hits found only through a type or status name (SR-068 AK5, #1807): after the property fields the engine has two more fields, `type` and `status`, holding the type and status names exactly as `properties` holds them (the empty text for a document without one, as above). They are never searched, scored or matched, so `properties` stays byte for byte as it was and every score stays the same to the bit. They only tell where a word was found: a term found in a document in `title`, `text` or a property field, or in `properties` more often than in `type` and `status` together (the same tokenizer, so the counts are exact), is found elsewhere; a word is found elsewhere when every one of its terms is (fuzzy and prefix matches included, SR-068 AK2) or when it matches an ID. A hit with a word that is not is late (`Found.late`, set by `scoreWords` for words, by the ID match, and for a phrase of several words by where `phraseInProperties` verified it); with `or` a hit is late when a word that found it found it late. Only a search of all properties can give late hits (SR-216 AK2: they include the type and status names); a single property or the title and text never do.
- Other designations (SR-220, #1809; `query/search-designations.ts`): a run of consecutive words outside quotes that is a designation of an element (its title, a synonym or its translation: the properties with the meanings `synonyms` and `translation`, D-059, of kind text) also finds the elements in which one of its other designations stands. The table of designations is built per index from the records of its store (`designationsIn`), in `prepareSearchIndex`, so a server's build and every carry (save, watcher batch) give the next search a new one; it is not kept in `search.json`, so a one-shot process builds it on its first search (25 to 37 ms at N-036 with 1,000 elements with designations, below). An element with an invalid status (D-044) adds none; a designation shared by several elements counts for all (also across a `distinct-from`, #1814); a translation may have several values and a Markdown one counts by its visible words (#1811). Runs are taken left to right, the longest at each place, never across a phrase (SR-220 AK1, #1817), on the words the search splits the query into, so a run may start or end inside a hyphenated word; a query word that a run splits needs all its words in the expansion as in the literal search, and its pieces are searched as words, not as an ID (#1822). The other designations are matched as open phrases (`phraseFound`: whole words, no typo, the last word as a word start, in one unit of a searched field), each one on its own and united, so a document is late only when every other designation that found it stands only in a type or status name (the `or` of `wordsFound` makes a document late when one word found it late). The rest of the query keeps its quoted IDs. The literal search stays exactly as it was (its hits, scores and late flags; scoring a run apart would change scores, since `scoreWords` multiplies by the number of query terms); the expansion is computed beside it (per run its words or its other designations, with the rest of the query), and a document it adds, or one that was late and is not late through it, is `other` (`Found.other`): after the literal hits, before the late ones, ID matches still first (AK2). Every hit gets the places of the other designations found in it, as phrases (several words) or word matches (one word), so the snippet and title marks show them whole (AK3). Without the meanings there is no table and nothing else runs (AK5). Rejected: expanding the query into one MiniSearch query (the other designations need whole words in order within a unit, and literal scores would change), and keeping the table in the search cache (a format change for a build of about 30 ms).
- Word parts (SR-221, #1843; `query/search-vocabulary.ts`): MiniSearch finds a word and the words it begins, never one inside a longer word, so the words of its term tree are kept as one string (`\0` between words) per MiniSearch object and change, and a term is found with `indexOf` from match to match, the word around each match read back between the separators; a word that begins with the term is a word start and is skipped. At N-036 the generated workspace has about 93,000 words (0.63 MB); a scan takes below a millisecond, walking the tree about 90 ms. The string is walked in slices at the end of a build, appended to by each carry (the words of the added documents the tree did not hold, taken before the old documents leave it; a word that leaves stays as a stale entry that finds nothing), walked again in `prepareSearchIndex` when the appended words outgrow it, and walked on the first such search of an index loaded from the cache (not kept in `search.json`). Only terms of words outside quotes with at least 4 letters and no digit look for parts (`withParts`); phrases, open phrases, other designations and the ID comparison never do, and `or` (MiniSearch's own search, used by no command) finds none. `scoreWords` derives them after the fuzzy words (a word part is no typo, so such a word leaves the fuzzy ones), weighted `0.25 L / (L + 0.3 d)` (below 0.25, every prefix weight above 0.288, every fuzzy one above 0.3), with the idf of the documents of all words the term derives in that field (`pooledOf`, computed once per field): never above the idf of an exact or prefix word of the term, so a part never outranks a whole word or word start only because its word is rare (SR-221 AK2). A scoring without parts stays MiniSearch's to the bit. `late` and `other` count a part as the word itself. `SearchHit.parts` names the words found only as a word start or a part, so the snippet and `titleMarks` mark only that part (AK3; also a word start, SR-068 AK2; a phrase, another designation and a typo stay whole), the window laid around the whole word. A page's matches are looked up per document until that costs as much as one pass over the documents of the derived words, which then describes every hit (a part may derive thousands of words). Rejected: an n-gram or suffix index (memory and build time for a scan that costs below a millisecond), walking the tree per search (90 ms, against the 100 ms of SR-108 AK5). Measured on the generated workspace with 6 compounds appended to every text (`test/perf/compounds.test-helper.ts`, about 83,000 distinct compounds; medians of 7 runs, container with 4 cores and 15 GB): the 4-letter part inside the most compounds (3,179 words) 65 ms for 50 hits, 135 ms for 500 hits with snippets, 100 ms grouped, 76 ms in the text, 144 ms with a word of every text; the most frequent tail 29 ms and 102 ms; typing it 30 ms per key; the first search of a part after a save 32 ms; the command prompt (titles with compounds, SR-108 AK5) at most 32 ms for a part. Before the idf was computed once per field, the costliest part took 260 ms (the sum over all derived words per derived word). The other rows on unchanged data stayed within the spread of the runs (build 8.8 s, 8.9 s; carry of one file 13 ms, 13 ms; every `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` and `[SR-108/AK-nqmj]` row below its threshold).
- `searchElements(index, query, options)` (consumed by `element.search`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#80, and the `[[` link search, SR-066): `fields`, `within` (the records of the selection of an active view or filter, `Selected.elements`, SR-068 AK3, compared by repository and file, so of two files with one UUID only the selected one counts, SR-088; `total` counts only hits inside it), `combineWith` (`and` by default), `fuzzy`, `prefix`, `offset` and `limit`. The words without an ID form are one query whose words must all match; it is scored by `scoreWords` (`query/search-scoring.ts`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#110, SR-098), which scores as MiniSearch 7.2.0's `search` does on the same index (the same terms: exact, prefix and fuzzy in MiniSearch's order and weights; the same BM25 expression and order of additions; the same `quality`; the matched words and fields of a page's hits as MiniSearch merges them), but in typed arrays per short ID instead of a result object per document and word, and describes only the page; it reads MiniSearch's protected index members, so the version stays pinned and `search-scoring.test.ts` compares it bit for bit with `search`; `combineWith: "or"` goes to MiniSearch itself. The record, key and repository place of each short ID are kept in arrays per index and engine change, and the first page is found without sorting all hits (pages up to 1,000; the order is strict, so the page is the same). ID matches come first, then hits that are not late (SR-068 AK5, above), then score rounded to 6 significant digits (MiniSearch's scores of equal documents may differ in the last bits between a fresh, a cached and a long-carried index, whose average field lengths are kept incrementally; the rounded score is also what hits give, so every access orders alike, SR-080), then store order (by the repository's place in the workspace file, then path, so no position of every record is built, #215). Only the page is described (fields and matched terms of each hit), for SR-098. `matchingKeys(index, query)` gives the keys of every hit without sorting or describing them, for the filter condition `text`.
- Phrases (SR-068 AK4, SR-174 AK6, #1806; `query/phrase-text.ts`): the index keeps no word positions, so a phrase is found in two steps. The candidates are the documents that hold every word of the phrase in a field searched (MiniSearch's term data, `phraseCandidates`, the last word of an open phrase by prefix, from one letter on; a phrase of one word is a word search without a typo); each is checked against the texts of that field (`findPhrase`): the title, the text the engine keeps (`SearchEngine.documents`) or one value of a property from the record, a Markdown text within one unit (`phraseUnits`: by lines, a paragraph with its line breaks, a list item without its marker, a table cell (also of a table right after the lines of a paragraph), a heading, a line of fenced code, and a deeper block quote begins a unit; Markdown and other non-letters between the words count for nothing). The indexed texts are not changed, so tokens, field lengths and scores stay as they were (SR-216 AK4). A document scores per field the phrase stands in as its words there do (`scoreWords`, no typo), and its hit names only those fields. The snippet and `titleMarks` find the phrase raw with the same units (link destinations as spaces, so the offsets stay), parse the lines of that place, and mark it whole in the visible text within one block; its words are not marked elsewhere unless they are words of the query outside quotes. The matcher works on any text and its units, so other fields of words can check a phrase the same way.
- `element.search` (`commands/element/search.ts`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#80, SR-068): resolves the selection (SR-068 AK3: a view, `where`, a package, IDs, later a saved filter), then awaits `searchIndex` and searches in the same turn, `within` the selection when it is narrower than the store or the index's store is another (swapped meanwhile: a new file the selection never saw is no hit); the hits are records of the index's store (a store swapped meanwhile is newer; the selection restricts it by repository and file), named with the short IDs of the selection's store. Output: one page (`limit` default 50, at most 500, `offset`; ADR-0009) of hits with UUID, repository, short ID, identifier, type, status, title, score, fields and matched terms, `total` and `incomplete`; no version or text. Each hit has its snippet (SR-174, `query/search-snippet.ts`): where a word was found and one line of at most 120 code points around the first visible match in reading order, the matched words marked, and the marked words of its title; the files of the page's hits found in their text are read again, synchronously, and only the Markdown line of the match is parsed, with the lines a hard-wrapped link or emphasis spans: a whole block (a list of 4 KB with links) costs about 4 ms with mdast, so 500 hits would take 2 s; one line takes about 0.1 ms (measured for pages of 50 and 500 hits on texts of 5 KB, also hard-wrapped, in `search-speed.test.ts`: up to 27 and 171 ms in a cloud container). A server imports the snippet module once its index is built, so the first search does not wait for the Markdown parser. The words compared are the hit's terms, so the snippet agrees with the index; Markdown properties are indexed raw, so a match only in a link destination there finds the element but gives no snippet. `fields` restricts the search (the `[[` search of SR-066 asks for `title` and `id`). Before the index is ready a search waits for its build (about 7 to 10 s at N-036, longer under load): every access gives the same result, never a partial one; SR-098 AK1 is taken to hold once the index is ready (warm server); the window after a start is spec question ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#388 (ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#110). The filter condition `text` (`condition.ts`) selects what `element.search` finds for its query, compared by repository and file. With `group` (SR-149 AK6) every hit is sorted (`rankedHits`) and grouped as `element.list` groups (`groupingOf` over the hit records), the search order kept inside each group; a grouped page of 50 rows for the costliest terms took 170 to 230 ms (median, cloud container), of which about 60 to 85 ms the sort of all 50,000 hits and 75 to 90 ms the grouping.
- Built per store and metamodel (`searchIndexState`, `query/search-state.ts`): the store keeps no text, so the build reads every element file again (`readElementBodySync`), in the calling thread, in slices of 5 ms with a turn of the event loop between them (as the current versions). A file changed since the store read it is indexed as it is now when it still holds the same UUID, else without text, and such a build is not cached. A change of the metamodel or layout resets it. A build or carry stops (`SearchBuildCancelled`) when its workspace is closed or its value is no longer the state, so a reset or the exit of a process never waits for a whole build. One that no search waits for pauses while a command runs on the workspace (`Workspace.busy`, counted by `invoke`; asked at every turn, then every 10 ms), so a command waits for at most the slice that runs; `searchIndex(workspace)`, which a search calls, lets it go on (the state itself is not exported from the block, since a command that awaited it directly would pause its own build). A search whose build stops because the state was reset meanwhile (a watcher batch that changes the metamodel or layout) waits for the next build, up to 5 times, while the workspace is open. A server starts the build only after its warm-up and one more second (`warmSearchIndex`, `SEARCH_WARM_DELAY_MS`), so nothing of the index lies on the way to its first answer (SR-082 AK1); it counts as a server from the start of the warm-up, so a search during it (`reqseq mcp` and the app do not wait for the warm-up) also builds without the cache.
- Carried over a store change (`carrySearchIndex`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#184; SR-007 AK2): the documents of the changed files are removed and the files that hold an element now are read again and added. Unlike every other carried state, the MiniSearch object is changed in place, not copied (a copy costs about as much as a build): the documents are read first and the object is changed in one synchronous step, so a search sees it before or after the change; the index of the previous store shares the object and filters its hits to its own records. A change of more than `SEARCH_CARRY_LIMIT` (10,000) UUIDs builds the index again. The engine keeps every document it added (`SearchEngine.documents`), so a carry removes the old ones exactly (`MiniSearch.remove`) and the carried index equals a fresh build, scores included. The carry is O(changed) besides the documents it reads (#215): the records by key are a patch of those before (`PatchedMap`); the arrays of the documents and their field lengths (`documentsOf`, `lengthsOf`) are copies of those built for the change before, with only the removed and added short IDs changed (MiniSearch's short IDs taken before `remove`), and the records by identifier a patch for the changed records; when the arrays before were not built, they are built in full at the end of the carry (`prepareSearchIndex`). MiniSearch's vacuum is never started (auto vacuum off): it walks the term tree in batches with pauses, and a document added or a search made in a pause (a search drops the words of discarded documents it meets) changes nodes the walk stands on, and MiniSearch 7.2.0 then throws in its timer (found by the SR-007 AK2 test and in review). An index loaded from the cache has its documents too (`search.json` holds the text of each, the rest comes from its record, `documentWithText`), so its carries remove them exactly as well and nothing is ever discarded.
- Measured on the generated workspace (50,000 elements, 200,000 relations, quiet local runs; `test/perf/search-index.test.ts`): build about 7 s with no turn of the event loop blocked longer than about 35 ms; heap about 200 MB with the documents kept for carries (about 30 MB of them), about 355 MB for a synthetic index of 50,000 texts of 150 words with its documents (about 310 MB without); `element.get` while a server builds the index 8 to 35 ms (paused, against 220 to 265 ms before the build paused for commands); with MiniSearch's `search` typical queries took 20 to 240 ms and very common words, two-letter prefixes and whole titles up to 400 to 500 ms; with `scoreWords` (ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#110) every term of the `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` test is below 60 ms; a carry of one saved file about 150 to 170 ms, of 1,000 files about 2.2 to 3 s with the store update (SR-007 AK2: 10 s). `element.search` through `invoke` (the first 50 hits, medians of 7 runs on a shared container with 4 cores, 15 GB; `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` in `test/perf/search-speed.test.ts`, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#110): before `scoreWords` two letters 264 to 337 ms, a word of every text 290 ms, a whole title 277 ms, three words of every text about 470 ms (single runs up to 860 ms); after it 15 to 60 ms for every term (two letters and words of every element 25 to 45 ms, three words of every text about 55 ms, typos and topic words 15 to 25 ms, IDs 4 to 14 ms), with single runs up to about 330 ms under garbage collection; all 638 outputs of `element.search` and the condition `text` for 51 terms in 9 variants (pages, fields, selections) were the same before and after. With the fields of single properties (SR-216, #1673; medians of three runs of the suite each on main and on the branch, same container with 4 cores and 15 GB): build 6.4 s before, 8.7 s after (ready after a start 6.8 s, 8.7 s), the longest block of the event loop 17 ms, 30 ms; heap 225 MB, 307 MB; `search.json` 31 MB, 44 MB, written in 1.3 s, 1.9 s and loaded in 1.1 s, 1.6 s; the synthetic index 356 MB, 401 MB (its kept documents 52 MB). These are recorded, no AK names them. The searches in every field stayed within the spread of the runs (every `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` term 5 to 53 ms, pages of 500 with snippets 165 to 198 ms, grouped 144 to 182 ms, the first search after a save 34 ms), and 232 outputs of `element.search` and the condition `text` for 29 terms in 8 variants were the same before and after. Restricted searches took 2 to 30 ms (`[SR-098/AK-pqx4]`, `search-speed.test.ts`: two letters and words of every text in the acceptance criteria, in the rationale and the text, and in all properties 20 to 30 ms, in the title 9 to 10 ms, IDs 2 to 5 ms). The carries kept their times: one saved file 12 to 17 ms, 1,000 files about 1.2 s (SR-007 AK2: 10 s), the search index settled 23 ms after a watcher write (18 ms before), every carried state 159 ms; the command prompt search (SR-108 AK5: 100 ms) stayed at 2 to 43 ms per keystroke. Phrases (SR-068 AK4, #1806; `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` in `search-speed.test.ts`, same container, four runs): three words of every text in order 114 to 145 ms, two words never adjacent 21 to 32 ms, three words across bold in texts of 5 KB 43 to 49 ms, open phrases while typed 57 to 271 ms (`"the s` the slowest at 192 to 271 ms: every text holds `the` before a word starting with `s`, so each candidate is checked against its text, and the scoring expands `s` to every word it begins), a phrase and a word 107 to 143 ms; with the texts in `search.json` (format 3) it takes 51 MB instead of 44 MB, written in 2.2 s and loaded in 1.9 s. The fields `type` and `status` (SR-068 AK5, #1807; one run each of `search-index.test.ts` and `search-speed.test.ts` on main and on the branch, same container): build 7.6 s before, 8.2 s after (ready after a start 8.2 s, 8.0 s), heap 307 MB, 314 MB, `search.json` 51 MB, 52 MB; the status name of most elements (`draft`) 34 ms, 38 ms, a type name that is also a word of texts (`requirement`) 18 ms, 21 ms, two letters that begin a status name (`dr`) 18 ms, 26 ms; the other `[SR-098/AK-pqx4]` rows within the spread of the runs.
