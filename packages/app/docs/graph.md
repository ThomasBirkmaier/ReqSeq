# Graph (SR-111)

Part of the [`@reqseq/app` README](../README.md).

- **Graph (SR-111, D-063):** one topic per line.
  - *Open and keys:* Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+G on an element or its neighbourhood (action "Show graph", the title of `trace.graph`, which it `covers`: the command prompt lists it once and its output opens this buffer, not a result buffer) shows the element and what hangs on it, in layers, as a buffer of its own ("Graph: <ID> <title>", reachable by Ctrl+P like any buffer); Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+G on the graph ("Show element") shows the element again. Not Ctrl/Cmd+G: Ctrl+G is the Emacs keyboard-quit (SR-121). Keys, while the graph has the focus (one tab stop, the selection moves inside it): Up and Down follow a link to the box above or below that is nearest, Left and Right go through a layer, Page Up and Page Down from layer to layer, Home to the element, Enter or a click opens the box as the current element (its element buffer; its graph is then Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+G). Keys with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt held are not the graph's.
  - *Data and modeline:* the nodes, their levels, edges, marks and evidence summaries are one `trace.graph` call (the buffer's element, depth, kinds, meanings, `withEvidence` and view, at most 500 nodes), so CLI, API and MCP give the same values (SR-080). The modeline says the depth, the kinds followed when some are chosen, the number of nodes, "more not shown" when `trace.graph` left some out, and "incomplete". A workspace change asks again and keeps the selected box while it is still there. A layout that fails keeps what the sheet shows and says why in the minibuffer.
  - *Layout:* the app only places the nodes (`graph-layout.ts`, dagre with the `tight-tree` ranker, the layout of Mermaid's flowcharts, top-down) and draws them. A link that runs against the levels (its target is farther down than its source, such as a decision above the element whose text references the element) is left out of the ranking and drawn afterwards between the two boxes, so it can never pull a parent below the element; such a link between two boxes of one layer is an arch above them.
  - *Ranked (up to 200 nodes):* every other link's target stands above its source (ADR-0004): what the element links to is above it, what links to it below (SR-111 AK1; for links that are not derivations this is the assumption of spec question ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#478). The drawn layers are dagre's ranks, not the `level` of the output: a node stands one layer under each of its parents, also across levels (a diamond, a link between two children). The graphs of `spec/` up to 200 nodes are ranked (on 2026-11-04 the whole chains of D-035, D-024 and D-002 had grown above 200 and are levelled); as `spec/` grows, more of the familiar chains near 200 nodes will switch to the layout below.
  - *Levelled (above 200 nodes, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#500, or when the ranked links would cross more than 800 layers, #211):* a link between two nodes of one level is left out of the ranking too and drawn afterwards, an arch when both stand in one layer, so its direction is not seen (recorded on ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#478). The layers then follow the levels (every node of a level in one layer, in the order of the levels) when every node links to a node one level nearer the element; with cycles through the element, a node may stand a layer off its level, but never on the wrong side of the element. Ranked, such links stack a level into many layers, and dagre then takes most of SR-111 AK6's second. A smaller graph can be as slow: dagre places dummy nodes on each layer a link crosses, so its time grows with the crossed layers. 199 nodes whose members derive from each other in chains of 20 within one level cross 1,863 layers and took about 0.5 s ranked (chains of 40 about 1 s), so a graph whose ranked links would cross more than 800 layers in all is levelled too (the crossed-layer count, an estimate of dagre's ranking taken before the layout, `crossedLayers`); the most of any graph of `spec/` was 462 on 2026-11-04 (the whole chain of D-038). `+` or `-` across either bound changes the drawing from ranked to levelled or back; the modeline does not say it (D-062).
  - *Either way:* every node of a level below 0 stands above the element and every node of a level above 0 below it (SR-111 AK1). A node whose layout links do not put it there (its walk reached it through a node that took its level from the other walk, in a cycle through the element, such as the glossary term GL-c736b0 in the whole chain of GL-f81ba9, ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#519) gets an invisible layout link from the element, or to it, which is never drawn.
  - *Look:* Boxes of 224 × 86 px over one SVG of soft curves (Mermaid's basis spline), on the white sheet with half a view of room on every side. A box has a line with the ID and the type by its name in the metamodel, the title on at most two lines, and at its bottom a line with the marks as words ("outdated" in the accent colour, "superseded" grey with the title struck through), then the verification of SR-136 as `trace.graph` gives it in the graph's view: a requirement's status word in grey ("failed" and "outdated" in the accent colour, as the matrix shows them) and a need's nothing; at the line's right end a ring of 12 px with its text, "2 of 3" for a requirement's verified acceptance criteria (`criteria`), "60 %" for a need's degree (`degree.share`, as the CLI words it), the track in the edge grey and the full part in the text colour. No ring where core gives nothing to count: an indirect requirement, one without acceptance criteria, a need without a valid derived requirement (`graph.ts` `ringOf`, `GraphRing.tsx`). The accessible name says the status and the ring's text after the marks. Its hover text is "<ID> · <type>", a line break, then the whole title, as its accessible name says them: the box stays 224 px wide, so a type that its line cuts (after a short ID of seven or more hex digits, such as "Component Requirement") is read there; the identifiers of `spec/` are never cut. The element has a dark border, the selected box a grey one. When the graph opens, the view stands on the element: in its middle as far as the drawing reaches, else with the drawing's edge one layer gap (56 px) from the view's edge, and a drawing that fits the view stands in its middle; the element is always in view (`viewStart`). A new layout of the same graph (the workspace changed) keeps the scroll, and an equal answer is not laid out again. Links are grey (`--edge`, 3:1 on the sheet; derivations darker), inline references dashed, outdated links always in the accent colour; the links of a box leave and enter its edge apart, in the order of where each curve is half a layer gap (28 px) from that edge: where it passes its bend next to the box, or for a link drawn afterwards on the straight line to the other box; ties by the same at the other end, so two links between the same two boxes run side by side. Curves of one box then seldom cross near it, but they still can (a curve that swings back after that height): on `spec/` (2026-10-27, 1,017 graphs) crossings fall from 102,471 to 96,147 and crossings of two curves near their common box from 10,025 to 5,039, and 6 graphs of depth 1 keep one, against 126 before (ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#555). The links of the selected box are drawn in the text colour only when it has some and they are at most half of the drawn links: a graph of depth 1 or a package graph, where most links touch the element, opens calm, and a box with few links among many stands out. No arrowheads, labels, legend or controls (D-062).
  - *Relation kind of a link (SR-154):* the mouse on a curve (a 12 px wide line without colour over each curve, so a thin one is easy to hit) shows a small text above the mouse with one line per link the curve draws, read along the arrow: the source's ID, the name of its relation kind or relation property in the metamodel (the key when the metamodel names none; "Refers to" for an inline reference, which no metamodel names), the target's ID, and "· outdated" for an outdated link (SR-025), e.g. "SR-111 Derives from N-071". The curve is drawn 3 px wide meanwhile. With the keys, `l` selects the links of the selected box in turn, in the order of the layout, and starts again after the last; the selected link is drawn wide and shows the same text at the middle of its points, which is then the listbox's active option (`aria-activedescendant`), so a screen reader reads its text as its name; its box keeps the selection's look but is no longer the selected option. Escape goes back to the box; a move, another box or a new layout drops the link. Tab is not taken, so it still leaves the graph. Otherwise no curve carries a word (`graph.ts` `edgeLines`, `GraphView.tsx`).
  - *Depth and kinds:* `+` and `-` show one level more or less (1 to 9, then the whole chain). The command prompt offers three more on a graph, with no key of their own (SR-111 AK2, AK3; `graph-choices.ts`): "Graph depth" asks for "Direct neighbours", "2 levels" … "9 levels" or "Whole chain" (the current one with the hint `+ / -`, which the action names too); "Show only relation kind" asks for "Everything", the meanings that `trace.graph` takes, read from its input schema in the catalog ("Derivations", "Replacements", "Successions", "Exclusions", "Inline references"; a meaning without a name here is shown as it is), then "Evidence" when `trace.graph`'s input has `withEvidence`, then every relation kind and every property of kind `relation` of the metamodel by its name, the key as hint, and the one taken replaces what the graph follows and whether it shows evidence ("Everything" is every link without evidence; "Evidence" is `kinds: []` with `withEvidence`, the element and the evidence below it, no link); "Also show relation kind", offered while the graph follows some kinds only or does not show evidence yet, asks for the same without "Everything" and without what it follows or shows already (also a relation kind whose meaning it follows; on a graph of every link only "Evidence"), and adds the one taken (core follows a link whose key or meaning is in either list) and keeps the evidence. So the evidence goes off with any entry of "Show only relation kind" but "Evidence", as a kind does. The graph is then asked for again, and the selected box stays while it is still there. The modeline names what is followed by these names ("only Derivations, Based on", "only Derivations, Evidence", "only Evidence", "with Evidence" for every link and evidence), and nothing when everything is. The depth, the kinds and the evidence belong to the buffer (also when it is opened again after a restart); a new graph starts at the direct neighbours over every kind, without evidence. Reading „Verweise“ as the inline references is spec question Q5 on ThomasBirkmaier/ReqSeq-archive#478.
  - *Evidence (SR-111 AK3 „Nachweise“, #217):* with `withEvidence`, each item of the output's `evidence` is one muted box (224 × 64 px, dashed edge, the raised tone) below its node, with a dotted line from the node's bottom edge, which shares the node's ports with its links. Its text is the CLI's line in the box's words: "evidence · 2 test cases, 1 record · 2 passed, 1 failed" (a count of 0 left out, the results in the order of the output), on up to three lines, and its hover text says it whole. dagre places it as a box of its own, joined to its node by a layout link, so it stands in the layer under the node and never on another box; it is not counted for the bounds of the levelled layout. The box is no element (the assumption on #217): it is not an option of the listbox (`aria-hidden`), the keys, Enter and a click skip it, it gets no selection, and the node's accessible name ends with its text instead. Core gives a summary only to the element and the nodes below it whose level + 1 is within the depth (so `+` can add the boxes of the deepest nodes); an element that is both a parent and a child at the same distance is placed above the element and so gets no box. AK6 holds with evidence too: the 500-node package graphs with a box below each of their 475 members are timed in `test/graph-performance.spec.ts`.
